## Decisions and rationale

### Accepted decisions

1. **Treat the full square banner as a composed lockup.** It combines subject, hardware, background, grid, and wordmark. It is useful for hero art, social cards, and presentation covers, but it should not be mistaken for the only reusable brand asset.
2. **Treat the raven as the compact identity primitive.** The bird can be reused without the wordmark or CRT when a favicon, avatar, watermark, or transparent subject is needed. The supplied 192-pixel image does not prove that the background has been removed.
3. **Keep the blueprint grid and horizontal stripes as structural motifs.** They are not decorative filler. They communicate protocol, categorical strata, and the industrial setting.
4. **Use rust-orange as the wordmark accent and amber as a controlled signal color.** The wordmark should not compete with the bird or become a general-purpose body color.
5. **Reconcile historical suggestions with current repository authority.** The historical palette and typography analysis informs future art direction, while `AGENTS.md` and `replit.md` govern site implementation.
6. **Preserve the canon boundary.** The locked visual and manifesto canon should be separated from living experiments, derivatives, and future prompt variations.
7. **Use the reverse-engineering prompt as a controlled design brief.** It should stabilize the visual variables and acceptance tests, not promise that an image model can reproduce every pixel.

### Rejected or unresolved alternatives

- **Generic crow treatment:** rejected by the manifesto lineage because the intended threat level is a mechanical terror bird that could fight a server rack.
- **Photorealistic rendering:** rejected by the supplied prompt because the target is a matte, etched, blueprint-industrial illustration.
- **Unbounded color drift:** rejected because the stripe order and rust-orange signal are part of the identity grammar.
- **Historical font list as implementation contract:** rejected because the repository already has a locked font contract.
- **Transparent favicon claim:** unresolved because the supplied 192-pixel image visibly includes a striped field and the binary alpha channel was not captured.
- **Pixel-perfect reproduction claim:** unresolved by design. It requires source-file access and a comparison workflow.

### Reverse-engineering prompt

```text
Create a square 1:1 retro-industrial OverKill Hill P³ brand illustration.
Use a mechanical terror bird inspired by a raven and a prehistoric raptor as the
dominant subject. The bird is perched on an obsolete CRT computer and keyboard.
Build the bird from layered, jagged metal feather plates with rivets, etched
inner lines, graphite and olive patina, heavy talons, and one controlled amber
or orange cybernetic eye. The subject should feel engineered, overbuilt,
watchful, and capable of fighting a server rack.

Use a flat, blueprint-industrial composition with broad horizontal bands in
deep industrial teal, moss or olive green, brass ochre, iron rust, and dark
graphite. Overlay a precise drafting grid and faint technical schematic marks.
The grid must remain visible but subordinate to the bird. Use matte grain,
weathered paper or drafting-film texture, thick graphite outlines, etched
crosshatching, restrained warm top light, and a soft vignette. Avoid glossy
photorealism, neon cyberpunk color, smooth plastic, and generic fantasy-bird
anatomy.

For the composed lockup, reserve the lower-right quadrant for the exact text
“OverKill Hill P³” in a heavy slab-serif style with a warm rust-orange finish
and subtle screen-printed grain. Keep the wordmark crisp, correctly spelled,
and separated into two staggered rows if the composition requires it. Do not
let the text overlap the bird's head, claws, or main CRT silhouette.

Preserve the visual hierarchy: bird first, obsolete hardware second, grid and
stripe system third, wordmark fourth. The result should read as industrial
nostalgia fused with digital precision, not as a generic logo or photorealistic
animal portrait.

Generate two related outputs from the same morphology reference:
1. a full square composed lockup with CRT, keyboard, grid, stripes, and wordmark;
2. a subject-only raven primitive with no CRT, keyboard, wordmark, background,
   ground shadow, halo, or residual gradient, suitable for transparent export.
Keep the head, beak, eye, feather-plate geometry, claws, proportions, and
patina family consistent between both outputs.
```
