## Decisions and rationale

### Accepted decisions

1. **MurderBird remains a terror-bird and raptor composite.** The current set consistently supports a large predatory head, hooked beak, armored feather plates, heavy legs, and talons. Future generations should not drift into a tidy crow, generic eagle, or soft mascot.
2. **The scene and the subject are separate deliverables.** A CRT lockup can carry brand storytelling, but it is not a reusable transparent primitive. Subject-only renders should be named and registered separately from composed CRT or blueprint images.
3. **Posture is a state variable.** Relaxed wings, alert assessment, wing-spread menace, and full occupation of the CRT are distinct states. They should be requested deliberately rather than treated as accidental inconsistencies.
4. **Material behavior outranks color labels.** The strongest shared surface language is etched, distressed, olive-bronze or oxidized metal with darker recesses and warmer highlights. A flat green color fill is not an adequate substitute for patina and fabrication history.
5. **Orange and amber are focal warmth.** Eyes and monitor screens provide the controlled warm signal. Additional neon accents or warm backgrounds should be treated as a deliberate variant, not an automatic enhancement.
6. **The blueprint edition is a named branch.** The silver schematic render can be retained as an engineered blueprint branch without redefining the olive-bronze MurderBird canon.
7. **The CRT must read as connected hardware.** Monitor, keyboard, vents, cable bundles, and talon contact form one visual unit. If the bird appears to float above the machine, the scene fails the occupation test.
8. **Notion is a canon sidecar, not the raw archive.** The fetched pages are useful for meaning, provenance, and gap audit. They are not a replacement for local asset retention or source exports.

### Rejected or unresolved alternatives

- **One universal pose:** rejected. The current batch demonstrates useful posture states that serve different purposes.
- **One universal background:** rejected. Green fields, dark vignettes, white presentation fields, and blueprint grids carry different presentation roles.
- **Treating all green images as duplicates:** rejected. Shared palette and frontal composition do not establish identical payloads.
- **Treating the silver blueprint edition as the material canon:** unresolved and currently rejected for global use. It may be a valid branch, but its polished pale metal differs from the olive-bronze patina target.
- **Treating any baked background as transparency:** rejected. The current images visibly contain fields, vignettes, grids, or hardware scenes. Alpha must be checked on a binary asset, not inferred from dark or white presentation.
- **Deleting the source threads after this artifact alone:** unresolved. The artifact is a semantic evacuation package, not a lossless Project export. Owner-controlled raw export or local binary recovery remains the safer archival step.

### Acceptance tests

- **Identity:** Does the silhouette read as a large land-based terror bird rather than a generic corvid or eagle?
- **Construction:** Can a reader follow the material logic from crest to breast, shoulder, joints, legs, and talons?
- **Surface:** Does the metal read as etched and patinated, with dark recesses and deliberate highlights?
- **Eyes:** Are amber or orange optics focal rather than one color among many?
- **Posture:** Is the intended state legible without relying on a source-thread explanation?
- **CRT scene:** Do monitor, keyboard, vents, cables, and talon contact form one believable machine unit?
- **Blueprint branch:** Does the grid support engineered meaning without overpowering the silhouette?
- **Asset role:** Is the output labeled as subject, scene, posture, or blueprint branch?
- **Transparency:** Is alpha verified from the actual binary before any claim of isolated reuse?
- **Typography:** Is the wordmark live layout text or a deliberately accepted baked lockup?

### Recovery and naming rules

Use lowercase, hyphenated filenames derived from durable meaning, not ChatGPT's timestamp titles. Keep source timestamps and original names in a manifest or provenance section. For each retained binary, record dimensions, color mode, file hash, source locator, visual role, and whether it is canonical, variant, duplicate, or reference-only. Never use a filename similarity as proof of binary identity.
