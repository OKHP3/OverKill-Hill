## Physical interpretation proposed for review

Use approximately 1.8 m floor-to-crown as a production target, with the same body dimensions across eras. This measurement is a proposal; the current story does not specify it. Changes over time are corrosion, repair, power and agency, not physical growth. Terror-bird inspiration does not identify the fictional fossil as a known species or establish its geographic plausibility. Species sizes varied; the Florida Museum's current Titanis account is a useful corrective to treating every terror bird as seven feet tall: https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/florida-vertebrate-fossils/species/titanis-walleri/

Show an adult on the same floor/depth plane and normal workshop furniture in the master reference. Establish scale in every scene before cutting closer. Avoid low-angle framing as the sole size cue. Both feet, their contact shadows and load-bearing joints should be visible in the reference. Use thick terrestrial legs, substantial hips, a deep hooked head and compact ornamental wing mantles. Avoid eagle proportions, broad flight strokes and a long sweeping feather tail.

Preserve the swept segmented crown, hammered ancient bronze, mismatched left shoulder, green mineral seams and irregular pins. Add Victorian iron straps and brass bearings only after restoration. Modern wires follow old tendon routes. The modern orange eye is restrained; do not suggest independent cognition in the inert water or Victorian repair scenes.

The first generated scale study overshot its human-height prompt. `scale-study-oversized-candidate.png` is retained as a design exploration, not a measurement reference. The revised `scale-study-human-height-candidate.png` visually places the bird at approximately adult height, with both feet on the floor and the CRT on its own bench. This is the preferred direction for review. Neither image is an engineering model or established canon; exact dimensions require a measured model or orthographic character sheet.

## Proposed story wording adjustments — not applied

The current text itself establishes tabletop scale in several places. Bigger imagery alone would therefore create a new contradiction. These narrowly scoped suggestions preserve the intent while allowing a heavy human-height machine:

| Existing phrase | Proposed replacement |
|---|---|
| flexible line that followed the bird across the bench | flexible line that followed the bird across the workshop floor |
| Wound springs carried it a few steps beyond the bench. | Wound springs carried it a few steps beyond the assembly cradle. |
| Bronze plates shifted over Victorian linkages as it crossed the workbench. | Bronze plates shifted over Victorian linkages as it crossed the workshop floor. |
| At the edge of the bench, the bird stopped beside the CRT and found two orange eyes reflected in the glass. | Beside the workbench, the bird lowered its head toward the CRT and found two orange eyes reflected in the glass. |
| The MurderBird occupies the computer now, with the casing bearing its weight and the screen holding its questions. | The MurderBird keeps its place beside the computer now, with the floor bearing its weight and the screen holding its questions. |
| Then a talon touches the casing. The file opens again. | Then its beak touches the casing, lightly. The file opens again. |

The last suggestion deliberately changes the gesture: a light beak tap is mechanically easier than lifting a load-bearing foot to a tabletop CRT. If the talon gesture is essential, stage an accessible low control surface and retain the original line; do not place the animal on the computer.

## Revised eight-second widescreen scene directions

Use a verified shared character reference with fixed height and proportions for every generation. A prompt alone is insufficient to hold exact scale. Each scene should have one readable action, one camera move at most and stable body topology. Keep 16:9, eight seconds and consistent frame rate for the eventual edit. These are production directions, not prompts already submitted to Firefly.

1. **Maker / purpose:** Ancient courtyard, human-height bronze body on stone floor beside the Inspector. Only ancient mechanisms. A single deliberate jaw closure below the hammer head leaves a shortened handle. No complete skull or modern parts. If this complex interaction cannot stay coherent, use a quieter inspection beat rather than accept a morphing hammer.
2. **Water / suspension:** Same body inert in a cistern, full silhouette briefly established against human-scaled brickwork and rope. Brown-black corrosion and green-blue deposits. Slow camera drift; no eye awakening, gait or wing motion.
3. **Mechanic / imposed motion, 1873:** Iron lifting stand over a floor-level cradle, adult restorer at same depth. Old bronze remains worn; distinct new iron/brass repairs. Visible external pressure hose. One driven step, no autonomous surveying and no flight stroke.
4. **Heart / capacity, 2025:** Brief full-body establishing view, then a restrained approach toward an already fitted ceramic-cell and capacitor cradle. Old shafts and cylinders remain legible; modern wiring uses existing routes. Components remain identical throughout. Avoid disappearing tools or devices materializing from hands.
5. **First choice / agency:** Full body on floor, technician withdraws from released supports before action. One uncommanded balance correction; head turns to the relevant loose bolt. No human pushing, flapping or giant stride. Stable hip, ankle and talon joints.
6. **Sentinel / chosen purpose:** Same floor-standing body beside ordinary CRT on steel bench; human operator pauses. Bird lowers head and makes one restrained cue. Operator resumes. Computer remains ordinary size and bears no bird weight. Preserve the quiet persistence of the ending.

The expanded 1853 discovery/transport passage has no dedicated clip in the existing six. A full life-story treatment should add a separate recovery/transit beat or explicitly present the six as selected moments. Do not label the excavation 1873. Six eight-second clips total 48 seconds before transitions; a 30–45 second website cut can select or trim holds without changing the original masters.
