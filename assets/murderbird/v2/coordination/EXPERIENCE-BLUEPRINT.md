## The page experience

**Opening:** the floor-standing sentinel in a quiet workshop, followed by the existing story title. Keep two clear invitations: Read the story and Explore its construction. Add a third, Watch the short film, only when an accepted film exists. On a phone use the full composition above the title or a separately authored portrait; a center crop of the wide hero loses the bird or its scale cues.

**Reading:** retain the existing chapter anchors and the entire prose. Insert a purposeful still at a chapter boundary, not between every paragraph. The current chapter navigation can indicate the active section and reading progress without scrolling on the reader's behalf. Every image has a caption distinguishing a story scene from an earlier design exploration.

**Construction exploration:** a reader-activated panel with three material layers: ancient bronze, Victorian repair, modern heart and mind. Use focused detail crops/art only where reviewed, plus a short explanation and a link back to the relevant passage. Keyboard-operable buttons expose the same information as pointer hotspots; a text list remains available. Separate Heart and Mind entries are essential: the story explicitly makes independence depend on both.

**Motion:** each accepted chapter clip is a deliberate eight-second event with a poster and a Play button. The water scene can remain a still. Only one player runs at a time; pausing or leaving the page stops sound. An optional condensed film is a separate edit, not all chapter players downloading together.

**Ending:** let the keyboard stop, hold the bird's attentive gaze, then resume the work. This is where the brand meaning lands. A small Find what doesn't hold link can lead into the projects or manifesto; avoid turning the climax into an unrelated sales overlay.

**Historical appendix:** keep the existing Visual record, distinguish original sigil / earlier reimagining / current production studies, and offer a gallery for readers who want the design process. Production controls, hashes and review status belong in that record or internal library, not in the emotional reading flow.

## Media score by narrative beat

All audio below is a proposed cue brief, not a claim that recordings exist. Build a reusable library of a few restrained mechanical and environmental sounds, then mix them consistently across clips. The bird should sound heavy and repaired, not like a screeching eagle or an unmotivated horror creature.

| Story location | Image selection | Motion intention | Proposed sound and dramatic function |
|---|---|---|---|
| `#the-maker` |01-maker | A measured inspection or one coherent hammer-handle event, not multiple morphing actions | Sparse hand-worked metal taps and courtyard room tone. Establish a small irregular bronze click that can return later. |
| `#the-water` |02-water | Stillness; if filmed, camera drift only, no eye awakening | A little water and cistern resonance, then space. No heartbeat or active machine sound. |
|1853 recovery within `#the-mechanic` |03-recovery | A single controlled lift or packing action | Hemp tension, timber strain, stone grit. Human handling moves an inert body. Add a recovery anchor in source if the prose owner wants direct navigation. |
|1873 restoration within `#the-mechanic` |04-mechanic | One externally powered weight transfer | Belt rhythm, a valve release and the old bronze click. Audible dependence on external machinery. |
| `#the-heart` |05-heart | A subtle powered test, preserving a fixed core layout | Quiet pump and old bearing sounds. No biological pulse implied by a sealed ceramic power unit. |
| `#the-mind` |Reviewed shoulder detail plus Heart context | Tiny correction within an asymmetrical joint; later create a dedicated mind/learning reference | The same mechanism, with a pause and more controlled repetition. Avoid an invented magical awakening chord. |
| `#first-choice` |06-choice | Hands withdrawn; one independent balance correction toward the bolt | Typing stops, a joint settles, a contact clicks. The order of events makes agency legible. |
| `#the-sentinel` |07-sentinel | Attention returns the operator to unfinished work | Familiar bronze click and a few keys resuming. A restrained resolution, not a louder climax. |

## Delivery behavior and budget

Targets below are implementation budgets, not measured final film encodes:

- Reading first load: no audio/video bytes requested until explicit playback. Load one appropriately sized hero; lazy-load later illustrations. Aim for under 1MB of initial page media.
- Existing 960px WebP candidates measure approximately 48–125KiB. Use `srcset`, dimensions and full-body scale context rather than sending masters to every device.
- Chapter clips: target roughly 2–4MB per eight-second 720p/1080p encode, subject to dark-detail and motion review. Keep a higher-quality master.
- Short film: target roughly 8–15MB for a 40-second web edition; verify readability and patina rather than pursuing bitrate alone. Provide a lower-bandwidth option if useful.
- Use native video controls, `playsinline`, `preload="none"`, a local poster and fast-start MP4. No automatic sound. A Sound on control must describe whether it controls a clip, ambience or narration; avoid one ambiguous toggle for all three.
- Captions include meaningful non-speech sound when it carries the scene. Supply transcripts for narration/dialogue and text descriptions for important visual-only events. Mute, pause, keyboard focus, reduced motion and a complete text-only reading path are part of the experience.

GitHub blocks regular Git files above 100MiB; that ceiling is much larger than a useful reading-page budget. Pages currently limits published sites to 1GB and has a soft 100GB/month bandwidth limit. At 10MB per film play, 10,000 complete plays alone approach 100GB, before other traffic. Those are planning calculations, not measured audience projections. Sources: [GitHub file limits](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/working-with-files/managing-large-files/about-large-files-on-github), [Pages limits](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits). Recheck the selected encoding and deployment bundle before release; do not assume LFS is the page's playback delivery solution.

4. Add optional chapter playback and the short film. Verify slow-network behavior, reduced motion, keyboard use, simultaneous player prevention, file size and no unsolicited audio/downloads.
5. Publish through one integration change; verify CI, Pages output, public media URLs/MIME, playback/seek, social metadata and the exact story revision. Source provenance and local existence alone are not deployment evidence.

The achievable first release is an illustrated story that already feels intentional. The later sound and motion should deepen a few decisive moments rather than make reading dependent on a large film loading.
