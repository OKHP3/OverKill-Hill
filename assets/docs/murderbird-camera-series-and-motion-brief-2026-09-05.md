## Proposed eight-second feature: The sentinel noticed you

Creative direction, not generated footage. Use the preferred head and one consistent articulated character across the entire shot. Camera low, with a restrained three-quarter view and bird/CRT dominating. No environment is needed beyond the host page's surface.

| Time | Visible mechanics | Synchronized sound |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0-1.5 s | Still sentinel over CRT. Optical aperture contracts; head turns first, body waits. | Quiet transformer hum, a tiny iris click, faint chassis resonance. |
| 1.5-3.0 s | Hip rotates over the planted foot, knee compresses, toes tighten against casing. Head stabilizes through a compact neck. | Three irregular ratchet engagements, stressed bronze creak, CRT case tick. |
| 3.0-4.2 s | Shoulder plates rise a few degrees as linkages preload; tail counterbalances. One worn joint takes up backlash before the rest. | Spring tension rises with a low motor whine; dry lamella chatter follows the load. |
| 4.2-5.0 s | Rear leg pushes against CRT; forward foot extends toward lens. Jaw opens through visible linkage. Body accelerates, not floats. | Heavy casing thunk, servo rise, short pressurized rasp through a resonant metal throat. No organic eagle scream or dinosaur roar. |
| 5.0-5.6 s | Beak and talon stop just short of camera. Torso decelerates; small plates and cable loops overshoot, then settle. | Sharp jaw clack, brief steel-on-bronze ring, delayed plate rattles. No glass-breaking jump scare. |
| 5.6-8.0 s | Head remains fixed on viewer while limbs recover under load. Hold a hostile, calculating final pose. | Motor winds down, one reluctant pawl clicks, residual hum remains. |

Movement grammar: hips initiate weight transfer; feet maintain contact until push-off; neck counteracts torso motion; load-bearing rods do not stretch; gears rotate only around fixed axes. Plates are rigid hinged pieces, not flexible feathers. Repairs introduce small delays, not random shaking. No flight, rubbery morphing, teleporting feet, boiling rivets or changing beak geometry.

Sound identity: mass before volume. Low hollow bronze body resonance; dry midrange iron mechanisms; only restrained high-frequency optical/electrical cues. No music necessary. Record or synthesize separate movement stems, align impacts to actual frame contacts, and review on phone speakers and headphones. This brief does not claim any historic automaton would literally sound this way.
