## Visual invariants for the new series

- **Scale and stance:** a large terrestrial automaton, provisionally about 2 meters tall when upright. Substantial hips and weight-bearing legs, a heavy head, a compact strong neck, and folded metal wings. A normal-height workshop bench and ordinary CRT provide recognizable scale cues. The floor, not the computer, carries its weight.
- **Hybrid identity:** the fossil fragments inform the stance and load-bearing body, not a known complete species. The curved hooked bill and ornamental wings are intentional survivals of the court's eagle commission. Do not turn the bird into a modern hawk or erase its preferred head to force taxonomic precision.
- **Ancient body:** cast and handworked bronze, irregular edges, hammer marks, flattened peened pins, and overlapping plate blades. Fossil bones were studied, not embedded in the mechanism.
- **Water history:** brown and near-black bronze with deep green seams and small sheltered blue mineral traces. Contact surfaces show mechanically motivated wear. Avoid uniform gold, chrome, or a generic flat green coating.
- **Left shoulder:** the plate fitted twice remains slightly asymmetric. The anatomical left wing folds within its more limited travel. This is inherited construction, not a visibly broken wing.
- **Victorian machinery:** iron braces, brass bushings, shafts, springs, and linkages remain openly distinguishable from the ancient casting. Repairs need not match cosmetically to be competent.
- **Modern heart and mind:** ceramic-cell core and capacitor ring inside the breast; pumps feed retained cylinders; sensors sit beside old pins; a compact processing lattice occupies the chamber behind the eyes. Modern conduits follow old tendon routes. No hose trails behind an active 2025 bird. The hidden charging contact and finite energy budget remain part of the fiction.
- **Light and expression:** orange circular optics, restrained glow, alert attention, and deliberate head inclination. The CRT remains an interface and narrative companion. Avoid a decorative glowing chest reactor.
- **Motion and judgment:** visible load transfer, running clearances, controlled joint travel, and mechanisms that support the pose. The bird identifies weak work, anticipates failure, and can apply force selectively. Threat poses must not redefine it as indiscriminately destructive.
